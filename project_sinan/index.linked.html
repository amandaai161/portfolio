01 About the project

The models got good. The briefs did not.

Image models got good enough that making a picture stopped being the constraint. What replaced it is harder to see: the distance between what a designer means and what an engine can execute.

If you have used one of these models on anything real, you know the loop. The first render is close enough to keep going and wrong enough to fix, so you add a word. Then five more. Twenty minutes later the prompt is adjectives contradicting each other, and the frames are drifting further from your head, not closer.

Then you need a second image to sit beside the first, and you cannot make one, because nothing anywhere holds the decisions the first was built from.

Sinan closes that gap by pulling the decisions out of the prompt. Two thinking skills settle what the work is claiming before anything renders. A direction layer turns that decision into every craft dimension it implies, so there is nothing left for the engine to invent. The make layer picks an engine on capability before price, and files a sidecar for every run.

That last part is what changes how the work behaves. The direction outlives the session, so the next frame in the set is written against a decision that still exists rather than a memory of one.

SinanInspired by

Mimar Sinan. Chief royal architect to three Ottoman sultans across fifty years. What he left behind was never a building. It was a method, plus a corps trained well enough to carry it without him.

02 The architecture

Five skills that hand work to each other.

Most AI workflows are one prompt doing three jobs at once. Splitting them lets each stage be good at one thing and honest about the rest.

Hands the work on Sends the finished render back as reference Think → Direct → Make, one direction only

Drag the map sideways

How a job actually moves through it

Three handoffs, one direction
Step 01 · Think

I bring a problem, not a prompt.

I describe it in whatever half-formed way it is sitting in my head. A small call gets answered on the spot. A real question gets handed up to the deep one, and we argue until there is a position I can defend.

Step 02 · Direct

The decision becomes instructions.

Only then does anything get made. The direction layer writes out every craft dimension that decision implies, and the make layer picks an engine that can actually do the job. I do not write prompts any more.

Step 03 · Make & keep

The decision outlives the session.

Every run is filed with its prompt, parameters and true price. That is why the second frame in a set matches the first without me rewriting anything, and why it works the same on a poster or a five-second loop.

Think · fast 01 / 05

sinan-brainstorm-light

The answer across a desk. One scoped question, one opinionated call. Fewer words, not less judgement.

The core strength

It refuses to be average. One test before it replies: if the answer would work for any brand in the category, it has not answered. So it picks one option, gives a reason that sits outside taste, and names what that choice costs.

Same question, two answers

“Fine-line illustrated bean, or the name in a heavy grotesque?”

A clean modern sans. Approachable and contemporary, works well for a coffee shop.

The wordmark. Your logo’s first real job is a 60 pixel circle on a delivery app and a stamp on a paper cup. Fine lines die at both.

The reason is arithmetic, not mood
  • Three moves, roughly 100 to 150 words: the call, the reason, the trade.
  • Assumes rather than interrogates, because a clarifying question costs the round trip you came here to avoid.
  • Never hedges with “it depends”. If it depends, it says on what, then picks anyway.
  • Hands off the moment a question turns strategic, rather than improvising a heavier answer badly.
Think · properly 02 / 05

sinan-brainstorming

The session, and the most valuable skill in the set. Positioning, territories, critique, and reading a board of references as a system.

The core strength

It refuses the question as asked. A brief rarely contains its own argument, so it works out what the brand is claiming and against whom, then derives every visual decision from that. Its rule: if two options could swap without changing what the business is, they are moods, not territories.

Direct 03 / 05

sinan-prompt-optimizer

The hinge of the whole system. It turns a decision into instructions an engine can execute: light, lens, palette, position, and what must never appear.

The core strength

It makes the calls you cannot make from nothing. Asking someone what lighting they want pushes the work back onto the person who came to offload it. So it decides, shows its reasoning line by line, and you argue with the logic instead of the output. Direction means choosing and excluding, not stacking superlatives.

Make · stills 04 / 05

sinan-generate-img

Routing and execution. It filters engines on capability before cost, rewrites the prompt into that engine’s dialect, and files a record of every run.

The core strength

It optimises for the first attempt, not the sticker price. The whole catalogue spans about eleven cents, so the model was never the cost. The re-roll was. Capability gates run before effort. Handed a raw sentence, though, it renders the raw sentence: routing cannot save an undirected brief.

Make · motion 05 / 05

sinan-generate-video

The same discipline plus motion, which fails in its own ways: aimless drift, everything animating at once, three actions in five seconds.

The core strength

It budgets the beat, and it says what stays still. Five seconds holds one action, so it picks the beat that carries the meaning. Then it names everything that must not move, which is the highest-value sentence in video direction. Straight out of the file:

One clip, one beat

Cost here spans 260 times, so the discipline is also the budget
  • Prefers image to video over text to video at every price point, because the keyframe fixes the frame first.
  • Spends the whole clip on one beat instead of three half-finished ones.
  • Names what must not move, which is the clause that stops logos and labels warping.
  • Has no maximum tier on purpose. Chaining video models compounds artefacts instead of improving them.

And the three things it refuses to do

The limits, on purpose

A system that answers everything confidently is not trustworthy, it is only loud. So the last part of the architecture is the part that says I cannot, and it is the reason the rest is worth believing.

Limit 01 It will not answer past its own ceiling When a quick question turns strategic, the light skill says so and hands up. It does not improvise a heavier answer badly.
Limit 02 It will not quietly substitute If no engine can do what was asked, it says so and gives the workaround. A silent substitution is the failure you find three weeks later, in print.
Limit 03 It will not present an estimate as a fact It quotes credits and dollars before it spends, then files the true cost after. Every number on this page comes from the second one.

Five skills, three handoffs, and three things it will not guess at.

03 In practice · Project #1

Resolving a brief that argues with itself.

A restaurant that wants to be premium and family friendly at the same time. Two words that pull in opposite directions, and an engine that will happily average them.

Meja

Worked example, run end to end for this page.

The problem, as it arrives

“It should feel luxurious and premium, but it has to be family friendly too. Warm, you know?”

Premium buys restraint. Family buys noise. Typed straight into a generator, that sentence leaves nine craft decisions blank and the model answers every one of them itself.

The prompt as typed

sinan-brainstorm-light The desk-side answer

I started where anyone would, with a quick question about materials. It answered, and then it stopped me.

Me, in Claude Code

Restaurant interior, premium but family friendly. Should I go walnut or oak?

sinan-brainstorm-light

Oak. Walnut reads as steakhouse and makes a room feel adult, and oak shows wear as patina rather than as damage. The trade is that oak is softer, so the tables will mark.

But this one is bigger than a quick call. Premium and family friendly are pulling against each other, and until you decide which one is the claim, every material answer I give you is a guess. Take it to the deep one.

Naming a contradiction is a five-minute job. Resolving one is not. So it escalates rather than pick a side it cannot defend.

sinan-brainstorming Positioning, territories, critique The deep one

So I escalated. This is the skill that will not let you keep a contradiction, and it starts by refusing the words in the brief.

sinan-brainstorming

Family friendly is a category, not a position. Which family, and what is this place claiming inside it? That question has four answers and they are four different businesses.

One question back before I take a side: what is the average spend per head? That decides whether the tables turn twice a night or once, and a room built for one sitting looks nothing like a room built for two.

sinan-prompt-optimizer Decision becomes direction

One decision, handed down. This is where it becomes something an engine can execute.

Me, in Claude Code

sinan-prompt-optimizer

Half of that is not direction, it is noise. 8k, ultra detailed, hyperrealistic, masterpiece, award winning, trending on artstation tell the engine nothing it can act on. Warm and high end but family friendly are the only two clauses doing any work, and they are the two that contradict.

So I am reading it as premium-warm, interior, mid-service, and deciding the rest out loud: 35mm at f/2.2 at standing eye level, low pendants plus one candle on the table, the diners behind left legible in the bokeh, a server crossing the near foreground, and the family mid-meal rather than posed.

Nothing quality-word gets appended. The last clause is the exclusions, and it is the one doing the work.

The direction it wrote

A general chat can rewrite a prompt. It cannot do this. This skill answers to a fixed checklist: every craft dimension is either decided out loud or flagged as left to the engine, and it always closes on what must not appear. A chat gives you a nicer paragraph. This gives you a specification you can reuse on the next frame in the set.

04 In practice · Project #2

Making a key visual out of an abstract idea.

A practitioner network with nothing to photograph. The position was already settled, so the whole problem was craft: say network with no circuit board, no hexagon and no glowing globe.

simpul

Worked example, run end to end for this page.

The problem, as it arrives

“We need a key visual that says we are a network. Premium, technical, not corporate.”

There is nothing to photograph, so the temptation is to describe the idea and let the model draw it. That is exactly what most people do, and it is why every technical community on the internet looks the same.

Typed straight into the generator

sinan-brainstorming Positioning, territories, critique The deep one

This one skipped the light skill entirely. There was no quick call to make, only a question about what the image is supposed to argue.

Me, in Claude Code

Key visual for a practitioner network. Everything I generate looks like a stock constellation. What am I doing wrong?

sinan-brainstorming

You are drawing the network from outside, like a diagram. That is a picture of the concept, and every competitor has the same one.

Put the viewer inside it. A network you are standing in has depth, occlusion and things passing the lens. It stops being an illustration of connection and becomes the experience of being connected. That is also a photographic problem now, which means it can be directed rather than described.

sinan-prompt-optimizer Decision becomes direction

“Inside the network” is a decision, not an instruction. Turning it into one is this skill’s whole job.

Me, in Claude Code

Key visual. Inside the field of points, deep violet, room for a headline bottom left.

sinan-prompt-optimizer

Treating this as a photograph, not an illustration. 85mm at f/1.4, so the nearest points render as soft discs and one mid-plane stays sharp. The hue goes in as a hex value, because violet averages to grey. Density falls toward the lower left, which is the negative space your layout needs.

Then nine exclusions, including the dandelion clusters that ruined the last attempt.

The prompt that worked

Two things a general chat would not have done. It specified the empty lower-left third before any layout existed, because it knows the frame has a job in a page. And it wrote the exclusions as a closing clause in the dialect the chosen engine actually respects, which is why the correction held instead of drifting back on the next run.

05 The controls

A creative partner, not an autopilot.

It is fast, it is opinionated, and it will still take a wrong position and execute it beautifully. These are the four places I stay the director, on purpose.

Authorship

The taste is mine, and it is written down.

Every rule in it is a preference I argued myself into and then made explicit. That is why the output is consistent, and why somebody else’s version of this system would be just as consistent about something completely different. Neutral art direction is not a thing that exists.

Judgement

I decide whether the idea is worth making.

It will direct a wrong position immaculately and never mention that it is wrong. In Project #1 it rendered family as chaos exactly as asked, and a chaotic room kills the premium half of the brief. Noticing that was my job, and the system is built so that the noticing has something concrete to happen against.

Selection

I make the final selection.

Eleven renders gave four plates worth shipping. Selection did not get automated, it got faster and better informed. Somebody still has to look at attempt 02 and see the dandelions, then say what to forbid next time so the correction survives.

Governance

I set the rules the system answers to.

Escalate rather than improvise. Say what an engine cannot do instead of quietly substituting. Quote a price before spending and file the true one after. Those are rules I wrote, not features I was given, and they are the reason I trust the parts that do speak confidently.

Closing

What I actually built here.

Three things worth taking from this, and none of them are about AI.

01

I found the real constraint.

The models were never the problem. The distance between a decision and an instruction was. That is a design-systems problem wearing a different hat, and I recognised it because I have spent six years solving the same one for developers.

02

I wrote the judgement down.

Not as a style guide nobody opens. As rules with reasons attached, in a form something that has never had a taste in its life can execute without me in the room, and refuse when it should.

03

I made it accountable.

Every run leaves a record, so the work can be argued with rather than admired. Two projects, twenty-five files, $2.63. Every number on this page was read out of them, not remembered.

A component library encodes decisions so a developer who was never in the room still ships something on brand. Sinan encodes decisions so a model that has never had a taste in its life still produces work I will put my name on. Same problem, different executor.

It started because I was losing whole afternoons to re-rolling, which is a small, unglamorous, extremely real problem. It ended as infrastructure: a method, a set of rules, and a record that outlives the session it was made in.

That is the part I would bring to a team, and it has very little to do with AI.
