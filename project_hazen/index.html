codenameHAZEN

The 5 skills that get a designer the image they actually wanted.

Most of us describe a picture and hope. These decide the craft first, so the engine builds the asset you had in mind rather than one that only resembles it.

01 About the project

The models got good. The briefs did not.

Image models got good enough that making a picture is no longer the hard part. What replaced it is harder to spot: the gap between what a designer means and what an engine can actually do.

If you have used one of these models on anything real, you know the loop. The first render is close enough to keep going and wrong enough to fix, so you add a word. Then five more. Twenty minutes later the prompt is adjectives contradicting each other, and the frames are drifting further from your head, not closer.

Then you need a second image to sit beside the first, and you cannot make one, because nothing anywhere holds the decisions the first was built from.

Hazen fixes this by taking the decisions out of the prompt. Two thinking skills settle what the work is saying before anything gets made. A third turns that decision into every craft choice it implies — light, lens, colour, and what to leave out — so there is nothing left for the engine to guess at. The last one picks an engine that can do the job, and saves a file for every run.

That last part is what changes how the work behaves. The written direction outlives the session, so the next image in the set is built from a decision you can still read, not one you half remember.

HazenNamed after

al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Haytham, whose first name reached Europe as Alhazen — and that is where Hazen comes from. He was the first to work out that we see because light travels from a thing into the eye, not out of the eye onto the thing. He proved it by building the instruments and writing down what they showed. Optics starts with him, and so does the habit of keeping the record.

02 The architecture

Five skills that hand work to each other.

Most AI workflows are one prompt doing three jobs at once. Splitting them lets each step be good at one thing, and honest about what it cannot do.

Hands the work on Sends the finished render back as reference Think → Direct → Make, one direction only

Drag the map sideways

How a job actually moves through it

Three handoffs, one direction
Step 01 · Think

I bring a problem, not a prompt.

I describe it in whatever half-formed way it is sitting in my head. A small call gets answered on the spot. A real question gets handed up to the deep one, and we argue until I have an answer I can defend.

Step 02 · Direct

The decision becomes instructions.

Only then does anything get made. One skill writes out every craft choice that decision implies, and the next picks an engine that can actually do the job. I do not write prompts any more.

Step 03 · Make & keep

The decision outlives the session.

Every run is saved with its prompt, its settings and what it really cost. That is why the second frame in a set matches the first without me rewriting anything, and why it works the same on a poster or a five-second loop.

Think · fast 01 / 05

hazen-brainstorm-light

The answer across a desk. One small question, one clear call. Fewer words, not less judgement.

The core strength

It refuses to be average. One test before it replies: if the answer would work for any brand in the category, it has not answered. So it picks one option, gives a reason that is more than taste, and says what that choice costs you.

Same question, two answers

“Fine-line illustrated bean, or the name set in heavy block letters?”

A clean modern sans. Approachable and contemporary, works well for a coffee shop.

The wordmark. Your logo’s first real job is a 60 pixel circle on a delivery app and a stamp on a paper cup. Fine lines die at both.

The reason is arithmetic, not mood
  • Three moves, roughly 100 to 150 words: the call, the reason, the trade.
  • Assumes rather than interrogates, because a clarifying question costs the round trip you came here to avoid.
  • Never hedges with “it depends”. If it depends, it says on what, then picks anyway.
  • Hands off the moment a question turns strategic, rather than improvising a heavier answer badly.
Think · properly 02 / 05

hazen-brainstorming

The long conversation, and the most valuable skill here. Working out what a brand stands for, the directions it could take, honest critique, and reading a board of references as one system.

The core strength

It refuses the question as asked. A brief rarely says what it is really arguing, so it works out what the brand is claiming and who it is claiming it against, then builds every visual decision on that. Its rule: if two options could swap places without changing what the business is, they are moods, not real directions.

Direct 03 / 05

hazen-prompt-optimizer

The turning point of the whole system. It turns a decision into instructions an engine can follow: light, lens, colour, camera position, and what must never appear.

The core strength

It makes the calls you cannot make from nothing. Asking someone what lighting they want pushes the work back onto the person who came to offload it. So it decides, shows its reasoning line by line, and you argue with the logic instead of the output. Directing means choosing and ruling things out, not piling up flattering words.

Make · stills 04 / 05

hazen-generate-img

Choosing the engine, and running it. It sorts engines by what they can do before what they cost, rewrites the prompt into the wording that engine understands, and saves a record of every run.

The core strength

It aims to get the first attempt right, rather than to pay the lowest price. The whole catalogue spans about eleven cents, so the model was never the cost. The re-rolls were. What an engine can do is checked before anything else. Hand it a raw sentence, though, and it renders the raw sentence: a good engine cannot rescue a brief with no direction.

Make · motion 05 / 05

hazen-generate-video

The same discipline plus motion, which fails in its own ways: aimless drift, everything animating at once, three actions in five seconds.

The core strength

It spends the one beat carefully, and it says what stays still. Five seconds holds one action, so it picks the beat that carries the meaning. Then it names everything that must not move, which is the most useful sentence in video direction. Straight out of the file:

Cost here spans 260 times, so the discipline is also the budget
  • Prefers image to video over text to video at every price point, because the keyframe fixes the frame first.
  • Spends the whole clip on one beat instead of three half-finished ones.
  • Names what must not move, which is the clause that stops logos and labels warping.
  • Has no maximum tier on purpose. Chaining video models compounds artefacts instead of improving them.

And the three things it refuses to do

The limits, on purpose

A system that answers everything confidently is not trustworthy, it is only loud. So the last part of the architecture is the part that says I cannot, and it is the reason the rest is worth believing.

Limit 01 It will not answer past its own ceiling When a quick question turns strategic, the light skill says so and hands up. It does not improvise a heavier answer badly.
Limit 02 It will not quietly substitute If no engine can do what was asked, it says so and gives the workaround. A silent substitution is the failure you find three weeks later, in print.
Limit 03 It will not present an estimate as a fact It quotes credits and dollars before it spends, then files the true cost after. Every number on this page comes from the second one.

Five skills, three handoffs, and three things it will not guess at.

03 In practice · Project #1

Resolving a brief that argues with itself.

A restaurant that wants to be premium and family friendly at the same time. Two words that pull in opposite directions, and an engine that will happily average them.

Meja

Worked example, run end to end for this page.

The problem, as it arrives

“It should feel luxurious and premium, but it has to be family friendly too. Warm, you know?”

Premium means holding back. Family means noise. Typed straight into a generator, that sentence leaves nine craft decisions blank, and the model fills in every one of them itself.

The prompt as typed

hazen-brainstorm-light The desk-side answer

I started where anyone would, with a quick question about materials. It answered, and then it stopped me.

Me, in Claude Code

Restaurant interior, premium but family friendly. Should I go walnut or oak?

hazen-brainstorm-light

Oak. Walnut looks like a steakhouse and makes a room feel adults-only, and oak ages into character rather than looking damaged. The trade-off is that oak is softer, so the tables will mark.

But this one is bigger than a quick call. Premium and family friendly are pulling against each other, and until you decide which one is the claim, every material answer I give you is a guess. Take it to the deep one.

Why it stops here

The two ends want opposite rooms, and every material call downstream sits on top of that. Oak or walnut is not really a wood question.

Spotting a contradiction takes five minutes. Solving one does not. So it hands the question up rather than pick a side it cannot defend.

hazen-brainstorming Positioning, territories, critique The deep one

So I handed it up. This is the skill that will not let you keep a contradiction, and it starts by refusing the words in the brief.

Me, in Claude Code

Fine. Premium and family friendly, and I cannot drop either. How do I hold both?

hazen-brainstorming

Family friendly is a category, not a position. Which family, and what is this place claiming inside it? That question has four answers and they are four different businesses.

One question back before I take a side: what is the average spend per head? That decides whether the tables turn twice a night or once, and a room built for one sitting looks nothing like a room built for two.

hazen-prompt-optimizer Decision becomes direction

One decision, passed down. This is where it turns into something an engine can follow.

Me, in Claude Code

hazen-prompt-optimizer

Half of that is not direction, it is noise. 8k, ultra detailed, hyperrealistic, masterpiece, award winning, trending on artstation tell the engine nothing it can act on. Warm and high end but family friendly are the only two parts doing any work, and they are the two that fight each other.

So I am reading it as premium-warm, interior, mid-service, and deciding the rest out loud: 35mm at f/2.2 at standing eye level, low pendants plus one candle on the table, the diners behind still readable in the blur, a server crossing the near foreground, and the family mid-meal rather than posed.

No quality words get added on the end. The last line is the list of things to leave out, and it is the one doing the work.

The direction it wrote

A general chat can rewrite a prompt. It cannot do this. This skill works from a fixed checklist: every craft decision is either made out loud or marked as left to the engine, and it always ends with what must not appear. A chat gives you a nicer paragraph. This gives you a written recipe you can use again on the next image in the set.

04 In practice · Project #2

Making a key visual out of an abstract idea.

A practitioner network with nothing to photograph. What it stood for was already settled, so the whole problem was craft: say network with no circuit board, no hexagon and no glowing globe.

simpul

Worked example, run end to end for this page.

The problem, as it arrives

“We need a key visual that says we are a network. Premium, technical, not corporate.”

There is nothing to photograph, so the temptation is to describe the idea and let the model draw it. That is exactly what most people do, and it is why every technical community on the internet looks the same.

Typed straight into the generator

hazen-brainstorming Positioning, territories, critique The deep one

This one skipped the light skill entirely. There was no quick call to make, only a question about what the image is supposed to argue.

Me, in Claude Code

Key visual for a practitioner network. Everything I generate looks like a stock constellation. What am I doing wrong?

hazen-brainstorming

You are drawing the network from outside, like a diagram. That is a picture of the concept, and every competitor has the same one.

Put the viewer inside it. A network you are standing inside has depth, things blocking other things, and things passing close to the lens. It stops being a drawing of connection and becomes what being connected feels like. It is a photography problem now, which means it can be directed rather than described.

hazen-prompt-optimizer Decision becomes direction

“Inside the network” is a decision, not an instruction. Turning it into one is this skill’s whole job.

Me, in Claude Code

Key visual. Inside the field of points, deep violet, room for a headline bottom left.

hazen-prompt-optimizer

Treating this as a photograph, not a drawing. 85mm at f/1.4, so the nearest points come out as soft discs and one layer in the middle stays sharp. The colour goes in as a hex code, because the word violet averages out to grey. The points thin out toward the lower left, which is the empty space your layout needs.

Then nine exclusions, including the dandelion clusters that ruined the last attempt.

The prompt that worked

Two things a general chat would not have done. It asked for the empty lower-left third before any layout existed, because it knows the image has a job to do on a page. And it put the list of things to leave out at the end, in the wording that engine actually respects, which is why the fix stuck instead of drifting back on the next run.

Twelve runs, four images used, and a written reason for the eight that were not.

One direction, three variants, three files. Each is the same prompt with a few lines changed: where the quiet space falls, how dense the field is. The set matches because it came from one decision, not three tries at the same mood.

The mirrored plate took two runs. The first let the points gather into a ridge across the frame, so no arc, no ridge, no crest went into the prompt and the second held. That correction is now a line in a file, not a thing I have to remember.

hazen-generate-video The same rules, plus motion

The plate had to move for the site header. Not become a different image, just breathe.

Me, in Claude Code

Animate the hero plate for a looping header. Subtle.

hazen-generate-video

Image to video, not text to video, so the image you already approved is the starting frame and it cannot drift. One beat only: the camera moves forward through the field at a speed you almost cannot see.

Then the line that does the real work: everything else holds. Field density, the bloom position, the palette, and the empty lower-left darkness. No flicker, no new elements entering, no colour shift.

Image to video · one beat

Fifty-six cents, and the single most expensive line in the whole project. Video is the one place where watching the cost genuinely matters: still images span about eleven cents end to end, and clips span two hundred and sixty times that. Which is why the rule is one clip, one beat, and a written list of what must not move.

Eleven renders gave four plates worth shipping, one clip, and a record of why the other seven were not.

05 The controls

A creative partner, not an autopilot.

It is fast, it is opinionated, and it will still take a wrong idea and carry it out beautifully. These are the four places I stay the director, on purpose.

Authorship

The taste is mine, and it is written down.

Every rule in it is a preference I argued myself into and then wrote down. That is why the output is consistent, and why somebody else’s version of this system would be just as consistent about something completely different. There is no such thing as neutral art direction.

Judgement

I decide whether the idea is worth making.

It will direct a wrong idea perfectly and never mention that it is wrong. In Project #1 it rendered family as chaos exactly as asked, and a chaotic room kills the premium half of the brief. Noticing that was my job, and the system is built so that there is something solid to notice it against.

Selection

I make the final selection.

Eleven renders gave four plates worth shipping. Choosing did not get automated. It got faster, and better informed. Somebody still has to look at attempt 02 and see the dandelions, then say what to rule out next time so the fix sticks.

Governance

I set the rules the system answers to.

Hand it up rather than make it up. Say what an engine cannot do instead of quietly swapping in something else. Give a price before spending and save the real one after. Those are rules I wrote, not features I was handed, and they are the reason I trust the parts that do speak confidently.

Closing

What I actually built here.

Three things worth taking from this, and none of them are about AI.

01

I found the real constraint.

The models were never the problem. The gap between a decision and an instruction was. That is a design-systems problem in different clothes, and I recognised it because I have spent six years solving the same one for developers.

02

I wrote the judgement down.

Not as a style guide nobody opens. As rules with reasons attached, written so that something with no taste of its own can follow them without me in the room, and push back when it should.

03

I made it accountable.

Every run leaves a record, so the work can be argued with rather than just admired. Two projects, twenty-five files, $2.63. Every number on this page was read out of them, not remembered.

A component library stores decisions so a developer who was never in the room still ships something on brand. Hazen stores decisions so a model with no taste of its own still makes work I will put my name on. Same problem, different worker.

It started because I was losing whole afternoons to re-rolling, which is a small, unglamorous, extremely real problem. It ended as infrastructure: a method, a set of rules, and a record that outlives the session it was made in.

That is the part I would bring to a team, and it has very little to do with AI.

Hazen’s real output

Never a building. A method, and people trained well enough to carry it.
